Canada Food System Stress Test

Canada's food system at the centre: stresses from inside and outside Canada, sorted into six categories, ranked, traced to where they hit, and tested one at a time and together. Every test compares acting early, acting late and doing nothing.

Illustrative only.Signal levels are the authors' demonstration settings and the models are stylized, with stated assumptions. Nothing here is a forecast or a live feed. Events and statistics are real and linked to their sources.

How the models work. Each scenario moves a shock through a chokepoint into the buffers that absorb it (stocks, inventory, storage, soil reserves, feed, alternative supply). Losses begin when buffers run out. Early action slows the shock, refills the buffer or reallocates it, at a cost that is counted. Signals mark when a watch, readiness or action threshold is crossed on the no-action path. The integrated test runs the selected modelled shocks in the same year, adds losses from shared buffers, and delays responses when agencies face several crises at once. The full program would replace these stylized equations with calibrated models, back-tested against 2020–26 Canadian events, and feed them with reviewed satellite, market, official and news data (see the user guide).
Framework terms (shock, chokepoint, buffer, decision window) follow AFI's food-system buffer and cascade-state work, and build on AFI's 2026 Chokepoint Audit. Contact: Arrell Food Institute, University of Guelph.
