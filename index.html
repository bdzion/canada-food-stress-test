Canada Food Shock Stress Test

A ranked register of shocks to Canada's food system, a simulation of each priority shock, and an integrated test of what happens when they hit together. Every view compares losses if we act early, act late, or do nothing.

Illustrative only.Stylized models with stated assumptions, built to show the logic of anticipatory action. They are not forecasts. Sourced figures and assumed values are marked in each scenario's assumptions table.

Shock register: preliminary ranking

Each shock is scored 1 to 5 on likelihood over the next five years (L), national impact (I), speed of onset (S, 5 = days) and cascade reach across sectors (R). Priority = L × I × (S + R) ÷ 10, maximum 25; ties are ordered by speed. These are the authors' first judgements; Phase 1 of the program replaces them with a structured expert ranking.

How the models work. Each scenario moves a shock through a chokepoint into the buffers that absorb it (stocks, inventory, storage, soil reserves, alternative supply). Losses begin when buffers run out. Early action slows the shock, refills the buffer or reallocates it, at a cost that is counted. Signals mark when a watch, readiness or action threshold is crossed on the no-action path. The integrated test runs the four modelled shocks in the same year, adds losses from shared buffers, and delays responses when agencies face several crises at once. The full program would replace these stylized equations with calibrated models, back-tested against 2020–26 Canadian events.
Framework terms (shock, chokepoint, buffer, decision window) follow AFI's food-system buffer and cascade-state work, and build on AFI's 2026 Chokepoint Audit. Contact: Arrell Food Institute, University of Guelph.
